# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Curious non-scientists exploring how an embodied fruit-fly brain may process sensation, reward, motivation, and action. Researchers and technically sophisticated viewers should be able to reveal exact neuron IDs, cell types, neurotransmitter evidence, dataset provenance, and modeling assumptions without making the default interface difficult to read.

## Product Purpose

Turn the MaleCNS connectome and its eventual simulation into an understandable, explorable visual instrument. The product should let someone see the whole male fruit-fly CNS, select a region or functional population, understand its evidence-supported role in plain language, and inspect the corresponding technical details. Success means the visualization is compelling enough to demonstrate publicly while remaining honest about what is measured anatomy, inferred biology, simulated dynamics, or an engineered interface.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, cinematic, lucid. The experience should feel alive without becoming sensational, and scientifically serious without becoming inaccessible.

## Anti-references

- Generic glowing network hairballs with no anatomical or explanatory value.
- Dashboards that overwhelm non-specialists with unexplained IDs, acronyms, and raw plots.
- Science-fiction interfaces that imply consciousness, emotion, or biological fidelity unsupported by the model.
- Decorative laboratory aesthetics, gratuitous neon, or motion that obscures comparison.

## Design Principles

1. Show the organism before the abstraction: always connect neural activity to anatomy, stimulus, and observable behavior.
2. Explain first, reveal depth second: plain-language labels lead; exact scientific metadata remains one interaction away.
3. Make evidence visible: distinguish dataset facts, literature-backed interpretations, model assumptions, and live simulation output.
4. Preserve causal legibility: focus and filter pathways instead of rendering every connection simultaneously.
5. Earn the cinematic moments: use light, depth, and motion to communicate activity and state, never to manufacture certainty.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the application interface. Do not rely on color alone for neural state or evidence class; use labels, patterns, shape, and luminance. Support reduced motion, keyboard navigation, legible minimum text sizes, explanatory terminology, and a color-vision-safe activity palette.
